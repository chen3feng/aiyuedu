# The Louvre Museum: A Temple of Art and Its Glass Pyramid

> 卢浮宫博物馆：艺术殿堂与玻璃金字塔的交响

<!-- 来源：Gemini 对话（share.gemini.google/6bKWnzs2Ol8h） · 原文 2703 字符 · 汉字占比 9% -->

Located in the heart of Paris along the Seine River, the

Louvre Museum

is the world's largest and most visited art museum. Housed in the grand Louvre Palace—originally built as a medieval fortress in the late 12th century—the building served as a royal residence for centuries before opening as a public museum during the French Revolution in 1793. Today, its vast collections span thousands of years, housing world-famous masterpieces such as the

Mona Lisa

, the

Venus de Milo

, and the

Winged Victory of Samothrace

.

Standing in stark, brilliant contrast to the museum's historic classical architecture is the iconic

Louvre Pyramid

, located in the central courtyard (Cour Napoléon). Unveiled in 1989 as part of France's Grand Louvre project, this massive structure made of glass and metal serves as the museum's main underground entrance, improving visitor flow while flooding the subterranean lobby with natural sunlight.

The famous pyramid was designed by the world-renowned Chinese-American architect

I. M. Pei

(贝聿铭). When his modern, geometric design was first announced, it sparked fierce controversy among critics who feared a modern structure would ruin Paris's historic palace. However, Pei's visionary masterpiece won over the world with its understated elegance and perfect transparency. Today, the Louvre Pyramid is celebrated as a brilliant fusion of ancient history and modern architecture, standing as an enduring symbol of Paris itself.
