# Grid-Scale Energy Storage: Giant "Power Banks" Balancing Renewable Energy

> 电网储能技术：构建稳定绿色能源网的巨型“充电宝”

As the world transitions toward renewable energy sources like wind and solar, our electricity grids face an unprecedented challenge:

intermittency

. The sun doesn't always shine, and the wind doesn't always blow, yet human demand for electricity never stops.

Grid-scale energy storage

(电网储能) acts as a giant "power bank" for the energy grid—storing excess power when generation is high and releasing it instantly when demand peaks.

1. Why Grid Energy Storage is Essential

Traditional power grids require a real-time balance: the electricity generated must instantly match the electricity consumed. Renewable power generation creates two main issues:

Intermittency & Volatility:

Solar power peaks at noon, but peak household electricity demand happens in the evening. This timing mismatch creates the famous

"duck curve"

in power grid management.

Grid Stability & Inertia:

Traditional coal or gas plants use heavy spinning turbines that provide kinetic inertia to keep the grid frequency stable (e.g., 50 Hz). Renewable sources connected through power electronics lack this natural inertia, making grid regulation crucial.

2. Major Energy Storage Solutions

Engineers have developed diverse chemical, mechanical, and thermal technologies to store energy across different time scales—from milliseconds to several days.

① Electrochemical Batteries (化学电池储能)

Lithium-ion Batteries:

Flexible, fast-responding, and mass-produced (benefiting from the EV supply chain). Ideal for short-duration storage (2–4 hours) and fast frequency regulation.

Sodium-ion & LFP Batteries:

Sodium-ion uses abundant salt materials, reducing dependence on expensive lithium.

Flow Batteries (液流电池):

Store energy in liquid electrolyte tanks. Highly safe with long lifespans (20+ years), perfect for large-scale, long-duration storage (4–12 hours).

② Pumped Hydro Energy Storage / PHES (抽水蓄能)

How it works:

Uses excess electricity during low-demand periods to pump water from a lower reservoir to a upper reservoir on a hill. During peak demand, water flows back down through turbines to generate electricity.

Pros & Cons:

Accounts for over

80–90% of global installed grid storage

. Highly mature, giant capacity, low operating cost, but requires specific mountain geography and massive initial capital.

③ Compressed Air Energy Storage / CAES (压缩空气储能)

How it works:

Uses off-peak electricity to compress air into underground salt caverns or high-pressure tanks. When electricity is needed, the compressed air is released and heated to drive a gas turbine.

Pros & Cons:

Excellent long-duration solution; advanced non-supplementary burn CAES (绝热压缩空气储能) recycles compression heat, raising efficiency up to 60–70%.

④ Gravity Energy Storage (重力储能)

How it works:

Elevates heavy blocks (concrete or brick towers) using excess power, then drops them slowly via cables to drive electric generators.

Pros & Cons:

No mechanical degradation over time and environmental friendliness, though structural engineering and footprint present challenges.

⑤ Molten Salt Thermal Storage (熔盐热储能)

How it works:

Often paired with Concentrated Solar Power (CSP) plants. Mirrors reflect sunlight to heat a liquid molten salt mixture (nitrate salts) to over 500 °C. The heat is stored in insulated tanks and used later to produce steam and drive turbines.

Pros & Cons:

Enables solar power plants to generate electricity continuously through the night.

⑥ Flywheels (飞轮储能)

How it works:

Spins a high-density rotor inside a frictionless vacuum chamber at extremely high speeds (tens of thousands of RPMs) using magnetic bearings.

Pros & Cons:

Extremely fast response (milliseconds) and virtually infinite cycle life, but short discharge duration (seconds to minutes). Essential for grid frequency regulation and smoothing instant power spikes.
