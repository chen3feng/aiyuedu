# Martin Luther King Jr.: Civil Rights Giant and "I Have a Dream"

Martin Luther King Jr.

(1929–1968) was a visionary American minister, activist, and political leader who became the most prominent spokesperson of the Civil Rights Movement. Inspired by his Christian faith and Mahatma Gandhi's philosophy of nonviolent resistance, Dr. King led a courageous struggle against racial segregation and injustice across the United States.

Born in Atlanta, Georgia, Dr. King rose to national leadership during the 1955

Montgomery Bus Boycott

, which successfully ended segregated seating on public buses. Over the next decade, he organized nonviolent marches, sit-ins, and protests against systemic racism. Despite facing constant threats, arrests, and violence, he remained committed to peaceful change. In 1964, at age 35, he became the youngest person at the time to receive the

Nobel Peace Prize

.

On August 28, 1963, Dr. King delivered his historic

"I Have a Dream"

speech on the steps of the Lincoln Memorial in Washington, D.C., before a crowd of over 250,000 people. His speech became an iconic rallying cry for freedom and human dignity.

Among its most famous passages is his timeless hope for future generations:

"I have a dream that my four little children will one day live in a nation where they will not be judged by the color of their skin but by the content of their character."

Tragically, Dr. King was assassinated in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1968, but his legacy lives on. His leadership paved the way for landmark federal legislation, including the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965, forever altering the course of American history.
