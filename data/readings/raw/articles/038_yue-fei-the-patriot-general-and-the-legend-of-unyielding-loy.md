# Yue Fei: The Patriot General and the Legend of Unyielding Loyalty

<!-- 来源：Gemini 对话（share.gemini.google/6bKWnzs2Ol8h） · 原文 7371 字符 · 汉字占比 1% -->

Yue Fei (1103–1142) was one of ancient China’s most venerated military commanders and a national hero of the Southern Song Dynasty. Renowned for his tactical brilliance, strict discipline, and unwavering devotion to his country, Yue Fei led successful military campaigns against the invading Jurchen Jin Dynasty before meeting a tragic fate caused by court factionalism.

1. Historical Background: The Jingkang Incident and Crisis

In 1127, the northern Jurchen Jin Dynasty captured the Song capital of Kaifeng, taking Emperor Huizong and Emperor Qinzong captive—an event known as the

Jingkang Incident

(靖康之变).

Collapse of the North:

The Song Dynasty lost control over its northern heartland, including the Yellow River basin.

Southern Relocation:

Emperor Gaozong re-established the dynasty in Lin'an (modern-day Hangzhou), forming the

Southern Song Dynasty

, while constantly fleeing Jurchen cavalry incursions.

2. Tattoo of Loyalty: "Serve the Country with Utmost Loyalty"

According to popular historical tradition, before Yue Fei left to join the army, his mother used a needle to tattoo four Chinese characters onto his back:

精忠报国

(

Jīng Zhōng Bào Guó

— "Serve the Country with Utmost Loyalty").

This tattoo came to symbolize Yue Fei’s lifelong ethos, serving as an enduring icon of patriotism and personal integrity throughout Chinese history.

3. The Yue Family Army and Counter-Offensives

Yue Fei organized and led the

Yue Family Army

(

Yue Jia Jun

), which became the main defense force of the Southern Song realm.

Strict Discipline:

Yue Fei famously maintained a strict moral code:

"Freeze to death rather than tear down a civilian's house; starve to death rather than plunder."

Anti-Cavalry Tactics:

The Jin cavalry relied heavily on armor-clad heavy cavalry formations known as

Iron Pagodas

(

Tie Fu Tu

). Yue Fei countered them by arming infantry with long-handled battleaxes (

Madao

) and spears to strike at the unprotected legs of enemy horses.

The Northern Expeditions:

In 1140, Yue Fei launched a series of successful counter-offensives, crushing Jin forces at the

Battle of Yancheng

and advancing near the former capital of Kaifeng.

4. Court Factionalism and Tragic Execution

As Yue Fei prepared to liberate Kaifeng, deep political divisions inside the Southern Song court cut his campaign short.

12 Golden Plaques:

Emperor Gaozong and Chancellor

Qin Hui

feared that a complete military victory might restore the captive Emperor Qinzong to the throne, threatening Gaozong’s legitimacy. They issued

12 Golden Plaques

(urgent imperial directives) ordering Yue Fei to abort his offensive and return to the capital.

The "Mo Xu You" Charge:

In 1141, Yue Fei was imprisoned under fabricated charges of treason. When questioned about the evidence against Yue Fei, Qin Hui famously replied that the charges were

"Mo Xu You"

(莫须有 — "Perhaps there is, or perhaps not" / "Unnecessary to prove").

Martyrdom (1142):

Yue Fei was executed in Dali Temple at the age of 39, along with his eldest son Yue Yun and lieutenant Zhang Xian.

5. Legacy and Posthumous Rehabilitation

In 1162, Emperor Xiaozong officially exonerated Yue Fei, restoring his honors and granting him a state burial.

Yue Fei Temple:

A magnificent tomb complex was erected in Hangzhou near West Lake. In front of Yue Fei’s tomb stand iron statues of Qin Hui and his accomplices kneeling in perpetual repentance.

Cultural Impact:

Yue Fei is celebrated in literature, opera, and martial arts tradition. The famous patriotic poem

Man Jiang Hong

(

River of Red

) is traditionally attributed to him, expressing his determination to reclaim lost lands.
