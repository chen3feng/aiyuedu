# Xu Xiake: A Legendary Traveler Measuring China on Foot

> 徐霞客：用双脚丈量华夏大地的千古奇人

Xu Xiake

(1587–1641) was a legendary geographer, traveler, and writer during the late Ming Dynasty. Unlike standard scholar-officials of his time who spent their lives studying for civil service exams, Xu chose a different path: traveling across China for over thirty years to explore mountains, rivers, and mysterious caves.

Driven by curiosity, Xu covered thousands of miles across dozens of modern Chinese provinces, traveling mostly on foot. His extraordinary life was dedicated to scientific field study, which he documented in detail in his famous masterwork,

The Travel Diaries of Xu Xiake

.

His contributions to geography were groundbreaking. He was the world's first pioneer to systematically study and document

karst landforms

(limestone formations), describing underground caves and subterranean rivers centuries before Western scientists. He also accurately identified the true source of the Yangtze River—the

Jinsha River

—correcting ancient geographic errors that had lasted for over a thousand years.

However, historical evaluations also note certain

limitations and negative aspects

. Living in a feudal society, Xu relied on private servants, mountain carriers, and local officials during his long journeys. Historical records show that on some arduous trips, his heavy demands placed severe burdens on local villagers and porters. Furthermore, his research was mostly limited to qualitative observation without using modern scientific tools or quantitative measurements.

Despite these historical limitations, Xu Xiake is widely celebrated as the father of Chinese travel writing and geography. Today, May 19th (the day he began his diaries) is officially designated as

China Tourism Day

, and many roads, parks, and landmarks across China—such as Xu Xiake Town in Jiangsu Province—are named after him to honor his incredible spirit of exploration!
