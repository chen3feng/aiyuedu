# The Origins of Weekday Names: Celestial Gods, Japanese Elements, and Different Starters

> 星期名字的来历：星辰之神、日本五行与不同的起跑线

<!-- 来源：Gemini 对话（share.gemini.google/6bKWnzs2Ol8h） · 原文 2653 字符 · 汉字占比 7% -->

Have you ever wondered why we have a seven-day week? The tradition comes from ancient Babylon and Rome, where people named the days after

celestial bodies

(the sun, the moon, and the five visible planets) and their gods. In English, Saturday, Sunday, and Monday are named after Saturn, the Sun, and the Moon. Tuesday through Friday honor ancient

Norse mythology

gods: Tiw (god of war), Woden, Thor (the thunder god), and Freya (goddess of love).

In Japanese, the names of the days are fascinating because they combine astronomy with Chinese philosophy, known as the

"Seven Luminaries"

(七曜). Japanese days use the sun, moon, and the five natural elements:

Fire

(Tuesday, associated with Mars),

Water

(Wednesday, associated with Mercury),

Wood

(Thursday, associated with Jupiter),

Gold/Metal

(Friday, associated with Venus), and

Earth

(Saturday, associated with Saturn). For example, Monday is

Getsuyoubi

(Moon day), and Tuesday is

Kayoubi

(Fire day).

Different cultures also disagree on which day the week begins! In many Western countries like the United States,

calendars

start the week on

Sunday

, which has religious roots as a day of rest and worship. However, according to the

ISO standard

(international standard) and in countries like China and most of Europe,

Monday

is officially the first day of the week because it marks the beginning of the school and work week. Meanwhile, in many Islamic countries, the week starts on Saturday or Sunday. No matter when your week starts, every day has a rich and ancient story behind it!
