Jules Verne

(1828–1905) was a legendary French novelist, poet, and playwright. Widely celebrated alongside H. G. Wells as the

"Father of Science Fiction,"

Verne captured the imagination of millions during the 19th-century Industrial Revolution. His vast collection of adventure novels, published under the series title

Extraordinary Voyages

(

Voyages extraordinaires

), combined rigorous scientific curiosity with breathtaking geographical exploration.

Born in the port city of Nantes, Verne was fascinated by ships and ocean travel from a young age. Although his father sent him to Paris to study law, Verne pursued his passion for literature instead. His career took off when he partnered with publisher Pierre-Jules Hetzel, who helped refine Verne's rich scientific research into compelling, educational adventures for general readers.

1. Major Masterpieces and Core Themes

Verne authored over 60 novels. Among his most celebrated works are:

Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas

(

Vingt mille lieues sous les mers

, 1870)

Plot:

Professor Aronnax and his companions are captured by the mysterious

Captain Nemo

aboard his advanced submarine, the

Nautilus

. Together, they embark on a thrilling underwater journey across the globe's oceans.

Journey to the Center of the Earth

(

Voyage au centre de la Terre

, 1864)

Plot:

German professor Otto Lidenbrock, his nephew Axel, and their guide Hans descend into an Icelandic volcano, discovering a subterranean world filled with prehistoric creatures and underground oceans.

Around the World in Eighty Days

(

Le Tour du monde en quatre-vingts jours

, 1873)

Plot:

The precise English gentleman

Phileas Fogg

wagers £20,000 that he can circumnavigate the globe in just 80 days using new steamships and railways, accompanied by his loyal French servant Passepartout.

From the Earth to the Moon

(

De la Terre à la Lune

, 1865) &

Around the Moon

(1870)

Plot:

Members of a post-Civil War weapons club design a giant space cannon (

Columbiad

) to launch a hollow projectile carrying three men to orbit the Moon.

The Mysterious Island

(

L'Île mystérieuse

, 1875)

Plot:

Five American Civil War refugees escape in a hot-air balloon and land on an uncharted Pacific island, using engineering and science to build a thriving community while secretly aided by Captain Nemo.

2. Scientific Vision and Lasting Legacy

Verne was remarkable for his

uncanny scientific foresight

. Long before they became everyday realities, his novels accurately envisioned:

Electric submarines (

Nautilus

)

Space travel, lunar modules, and splashdown landings in the ocean

Scuba diving equipment and directed-energy weapons

Video conferencing, news broadcasts, and skywriting

Verne's works inspired generations of real-world scientists, inventors, and explorers—including submarine pioneer Simon Lake, polar explorer Richard Byrd, and rocket scientists Wernher von Braun and Konstantin Tsiolkovsky. Today, he remains one of the most translated authors in human history.
