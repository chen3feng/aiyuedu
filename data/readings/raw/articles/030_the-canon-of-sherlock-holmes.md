# The Canon of Sherlock Holmes

The Canon of Sherlock Holmes

is a world-renowned collection of detective stories written by the British author and physician

Sir Arthur Conan Doyle

. Featuring the iconic "consulting detective" Sherlock Holmes and his loyal companion Dr. John Watson, these works established the gold standard for classic detective fiction.

1. Structure of the Canon

The official Sherlock Holmes canon written by Arthur Conan Doyle consists of

4 novels

and

56 short stories

(published across 5 collections), spanning from 1887 to 1927.

The 4 Novels

A Study in Scarlet (1887)

Holmes and Watson meet for the first time at St. Bartholomew's Hospital in London and become roommates at 221B Baker Street. The case involves a complex tale of revenge originating in the American West.

The Sign of the Four (1890)

A mystery centered around the Agra treasure and British colonial history, during which Dr. Watson meets his future wife, Mary Morstan.

The Hound of the Baskervilles (1902)

The most famous gothic masterpiece in the canon, investigating a deadly family curse involving a giant spectral hound on the Dartmoor fog-bound moors.

The Valley of Fear (1915)

Unveils the sinister shadow of Holmes's arch-nemesis, Professor Moriarty, featuring a locked-room murder in England tied to a secret society in America.

The 5 Short Story Collections

The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes (1892)

(12 stories): Features classic tales like

"A Scandal in Bohemia"

(introducing Irene Adler),

"The Red-Headed League"

, and

"The Speckled Band"

.

The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes (1894)

(12 stories): Concludes with

"The Final Problem"

, where Holmes seemingly dies at Reichenbach Falls battling Professor Moriarty.

The Return of Sherlock Holmes (1905)

(13 stories): Following public outcry, Doyle revived the detective in

"The Adventure of the Empty House"

, explaining how Holmes survived.

His Last Bow (1917)

(7/8 stories): Includes wartime spy cases, climaxing in the title story set on the eve of World War I.

The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes (1927)

(12 stories): The final collection of stories published during Doyle's later years.

2. Core Characters

Sherlock Holmes:

The world's only "consulting detective," renowned for his mastery of

deductive reasoning

, forensic science, chemistry, and disguise.

Dr. John H. Watson:

An army surgeon wounded in Afghanistan who serves as Holmes's flatmate, assistant, and chronicler.

Professor James Moriarty:

The "Napoleon of Crime," a brilliant mathematics professor who operates a vast underground criminal network.

Irene Adler:

A talented opera singer who outsmarts Holmes in

"A Scandal in Bohemia"

, forever remembered by Holmes as "The Woman."

Mycroft Holmes:

Sherlock's elder brother, who possesses even greater powers of observation and holds a high-ranking position in the British government.

Inspector Lestrade:

A dedicated Scotland Yard detective who frequently consults Holmes on difficult cases.

3. Impact and Literary Legacy

Pioneer of Deductive Method:

Inspired by his medical professor Dr. Joseph Bell, Conan Doyle introduced precise observation and logical deduction to criminal investigation, shaping modern forensic fiction.

Victorian Atmosphere:

The stories vivid portray late-Victorian and Edwardian London—complete with fog-choked streets, hansom cabs, clay pipes, and rich social atmosphere.

Cultural Icons:

The deerstalker hat, pipe, magnifying glass, and the address

221B Baker Street

remain immortal symbols of mystery and intellect worldwide.
