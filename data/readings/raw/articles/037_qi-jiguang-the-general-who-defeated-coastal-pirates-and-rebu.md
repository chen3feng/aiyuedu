# Qi Jiguang: The General Who Defeated Coastal Pirates and Rebuilt the Great Wall

<!-- 来源：Gemini 对话（share.gemini.google/6bKWnzs2Ol8h） · 原文 7995 字符 · 汉字占比 2% -->

Qi Jiguang (1528–1588) was one of the Ming Dynasty’s most celebrated military commanders, strategists, and engineers. Renowned for clearing China’s southeastern coast of pirate raids (

Wokou

) and later fortifying the northern frontier against nomadic invasions, Qi redefined early modern Chinese warfare through rigorous discipline, innovative tactics, and pioneering firearms integration.

1. Historical Background: The Crisis of the Wokou

During the 16th century (Mid-Ming Dynasty), China’s southeastern coast—spanning Zhejiang, Fujian, and Guangdong—was devastated by the

Wokou

(coastal pirates).

Military Decay:

The traditional Ming hereditary military units (

Weisuo

) were corrupt, undertrained, and regularly routed by pirate forces.

Tactical Mismatch:

The Wokou favored aggressive, close-quarters combat with Japanese katana blades (

nodachi

) and quick ambush maneuvers that dismantled conventional Ming infantry formations.

2. The Qi Family Army and Tactical Innovations

Assigned to Shandong and later Zhejiang in the 1550s, Qi Jiguang recognized that military reform required both fresh recruits and completely redesigned squad-level tactics.

Building the "Qi Family Army" (

Qi Jia Jun

)

Qi bypassed corrupt garrison troops and recruited over 3,000 sturdy, disciplined miners and farmers from Yiwu, Zhejiang. He instituted strict military codes, transparent merit rewards, and rigorous collective conditioning.

The Mandarin Duck Formation (

Yuan Yang Zhen

)

To counter the high lethal dexterity of pirate swordsmen in narrow southern terrains, Qi created the

Mandarin Duck Formation

—a flexible 12-person squad utilizing combined arms where every soldier fulfilled a distinct defensive or offensive role.

Shields (

Tengpai

):

Two soldiers with large rattan or wooden shields absorbed arrows and blocked sword strikes.

Wolf Bamboo Shoots (

Langya Bang

):

Two soldiers wielded multi-branched bamboo stalks fitted with iron tips to entangle enemy blades and obstruct charging swordfighters.

Long Spears (

Changqiang

):

Four spearmen delivered deadly strikes over the bamboo barrier.

Tridents/Short Weapons:

Two soldiers guarded the rear and flanks, utilizing tridents or fire-lance firearms.

3. Major Victories and Coastal Pacification

Between 1561 and 1567, Qi Jiguang led his army in a series of decisive, low-casualty campaigns across southeastern China.

Battle of Taizhou (1561):

Qi's forces engaged multiple pirate columns across Zhejiang, achieving nine consecutive victories and virtually eradicating the Wokou threat in the province.

Campaigns in Fujian and Guangdong:

Coordinating with naval commander Yu Dayou, Qi dismantled major pirate strongholds in coastal islands like Linjiang and Nan'ao, restoring maritime trade security.

4. Northern Frontier Defense: Rebuilding the Great Wall

In 1568, the Ming court reassigned Qi Jiguang to the Jianguan northern front near Beijing to protect the capital against Mongol invasions led by Altan Khan.

Structural Innovation (Watchtowers):

Qi designed and constructed thousands of hollow, two-to-three-story brick and stone

watchtowers

(

Daitoulou

) along the Great Wall (including famous sections like Jushanling and Badaling). These towers served as self-contained garrisons housing troops, weapons, food, and signal beacons.

Firearms & Wagon Squadrons:

On the open northern plains, Qi developed mobile wagon forts equipped with cannons (

Folangji

cannons and matchlocks) to repel heavy cavalry charges.

5. Military Treatises and Historical Significance

Qi Jiguang was also a prolific author whose military texts influenced Asian warfare for centuries:

Ji Xiao Xin Shu

(新书 /

New Treatise on Military Efficiency

):

A practical manual detailing squad tactics, weapon specifications, physical training, and unit discipline.

Lian Bing Zhi Za

(练兵实纪 /

Practical Notes on Troop Training

):

A comprehensive operational guide focusing on northern border logistics, artillery deployment, and command structures.

Qi Jiguang’s strategic brilliance combined empirical observation with rigorous engineering, making him a timeless symbol of patriotism and military innovation.
