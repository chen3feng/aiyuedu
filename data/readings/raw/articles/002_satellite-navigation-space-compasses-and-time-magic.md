# Satellite Navigation: Space Compasses and Time Magic

> 卫星定位系统：太空中的“指南针”与时间魔法

<!-- 来源：Gemini 对话（share.gemini.google/6bKWnzs2Ol8h） · 原文 2886 字符 · 汉字占比 8% -->

Have you ever used maps on a phone to find a restaurant? Satellite navigation systems make this easy by telling us exactly where we are on Earth.

How do they work? The idea is actually simple: distance equals speed multiplied by time. Satellites high above the Earth constantly broadcast radio signals. These signals travel at the speed of light. Your phone receives signals from at least four satellites and measures how long they took to arrive. By calculating these tiny time differences, your phone can pinpoint your location on Earth!

Because time must be super accurate, satellites carry

atomic clocks

. They are so precise that they lose only one second every millions of years. But here is the amazing part: scientists must use Albert Einstein's

Theory of Relativity

to adjust these clocks! Because satellites move fast and are far away from Earth's gravity, their clocks run about 38 microseconds faster than clocks on the ground every day. Without relativistic corrections, your map app would be wrong by several kilometers each day!

The most famous system is the United States'

GPS

(Global Positioning System). However, other countries have built their own systems too. China has built the powerful

Baidu

(BDS) system, Europe uses

Galileo

, and Russia relies on

GLONASS

. Today, smart devices often use signals from all these systems together to give us faster and more accurate positioning.
