# The Origins of Month Names: Marks of Roman Mythology and History

> 各个月份名字的来历：古罗马神话与历史的印记

<!-- 来源：Gemini 对话（share.gemini.google/6bKWnzs2Ol8h） · 原文 2569 字符 · 汉字占比 6% -->

Have you ever wondered why January comes first, or why September means the ninth month even though its name sounds like "seven"? The names of our twelve months come mostly from ancient Rome, blending gods, emperors, and old Roman numbers.

In the early Roman calendar, the year had only 10 months and began in March.

January

(Janus) is named after the Roman god of gates, beginnings, and transitions, who had two faces looking forward and backward.

February

comes from

februa

, an ancient Roman festival of purification.

March

honors Mars, the Roman god of war.

April

likely comes from a Latin word meaning "to open," celebrating flowers opening in spring.

May

is named after Maia, the goddess of growth and spring, while

June

honors Juno, the queen of the gods.

The later months carry numbers from the old Roman calendar.

September

comes from

septem

(seven),

October

from

octo

(eight),

November

from

novem

(nine), and

December

from

decem

(ten). Even though Julius Caesar and Augustus added January and February to the start of the year—shifting the numbered months to positions 9 through 12—their original names stayed the same!

Finally, the summer months immortalize two powerful Roman rulers.

July

is named after Julius Caesar, who reformed the calendar, and

August

is named after his adopted son Augustus, the first Roman emperor. Every time we check a calendar, we are reading a story thousands of years old!
