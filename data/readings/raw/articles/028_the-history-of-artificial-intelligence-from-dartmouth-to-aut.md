# The History of Artificial Intelligence: From Dartmouth to Autonomous Agents and Robotics

> 人工智能发展史：从达特茅斯到智能体与机器人

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has evolved from an ambitious academic dream into the defining general-purpose technology of the 21st century. Its history is marked by cycles of breakthrough innovations and "AI winters," leading up to today's generative AI revolution.

1. The Dartmouth Conference (1956)

The official birth of AI as an academic discipline occurred at the

Dartmouth Conference

in the summer of 1956. Organized by visionaries

John McCarthy

(who coined the term "Artificial Intelligence"),

Marvin Minsky

,

Nathaniel Rochester

, and

Claude Shannon

, the conference operated on the core hypothesis that every aspect of learning or intelligence could be precisely described so that a machine could simulate it.

2. Expert Systems & Symbolism

In the 1970s and 1980s, AI was dominated by

Symbolic AI

and

Expert Systems

(e.g., MYCIN and DENDRAL). These systems relied on hand-crafted "if-then" rules written by human domain experts. While successful in narrow domains like medical diagnosis or chess, expert systems suffered from rigidity—they couldn't handle real-world ambiguity or learn independently, leading to the second "AI Winter."

3. Machine Learning & Statistical Methods

By the late 1990s and 2000s, the paradigm shifted to

Machine Learning (ML)

. Instead of hardcoding rules, researchers used statistical algorithms (such as Decision Trees, Support Vector Machines, and Random Forests) that allowed computers to infer patterns directly from data.

4. Neural Networks and Deep Learning

Inspired by the human brain, artificial neural networks were refined by pioneers like

Geoffrey Hinton

,

Yann LeCun

, and

Yoshua Bengio

(the "Godfathers of Deep Learning"). In 2012, the breakthrough

AlexNet

model crushed competition in the ImageNet challenge using

Deep Learning

—multi-layered neural networks accelerated by Graphics Processing Units (GPUs). Deep learning rapidly revolutionized computer vision and speech recognition.

5. The Transformer Architecture (2017)

In 2017, Google researchers published the landmark paper

"Attention Is All You Need,"

introducing the

Transformer

architecture. By replacing recurrent connections with self-attention mechanisms, Transformers allowed parallel processing of massive text datasets, paving the way for modern Large Language Models (LLMs).

6. ChatGPT & the AIGC Explosion

In late 2022, OpenAI released

ChatGPT

, bringing conversational AI into mainstream global consciousness. This marked the birth of the

AIGC (AI-Generated Content)

era, moving AI from passive recognition (image classification) to active creation (generating text, photo-realistic images, music, and synthetic video).

7. AI Coding, Agents, World Models & Robotics

Modern AI has expanded into specialized operational frontiers:

AI Coding:

Tools like GitHub Copilot, Cursor, and Claude Code transform software development by auto-generating, debugging, and refactoring complex codebases.

AI Agents (智能体):

Moving beyond single-turn chats, autonomous agents can break down multi-step goals, call external tools, use operating systems, and execute complex workflows independently.

World Models & Physical AI (世界模型与机器人):

Pioneers in Embodied AI merge generative models with physical robotics (e.g., Tesla Optimus, Figure AI, and Boston Dynamics). World models allow AI to understand physical spatial dynamics, enabling humanoid robots to perceive, reason, and navigate physical human environments.

9. Impact on Human Society

Productivity Transformation:

Automates routine tasks in coding, writing, research, and data analysis, accelerating human creativity while shifting workplace demands.

Scientific Breakthroughs:

Accelerates drug discovery, protein folding prediction (AlphaFold), material science, and climate modeling.

Ethical & Societal Challenges:

Deepens concerns regarding job displacement, copyright ownership, AI safety/alignment, deepfakes, energy consumption of datacenters, and digital inequality.
