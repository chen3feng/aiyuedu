# Jianzhen's Voyages to Japan: Six Trials and a Bridge of Cultural Exchange

> 鉴真东渡：六折不挠的弘法传奇与中日文化桥梁

Jianzhen

(688–763) was a high-ranking Buddhist monk of the Tang Dynasty whose unwavering determination left a legendary mark on East Asian history. In the 8th century, Japanese monks traveled to China to invite a master who could establish proper Buddhist ordination rules in Japan. Master Jianzhen accepted the challenging mission, declaring that he would sacrifice his life for the sake of the Dharma.

Accomplishing this journey was extraordinarily dangerous. Crossing the stormy East China Sea in small wooden ships meant facing deadly typhoons, shipwrecks, and pirate attacks. Jianzhen attempted the voyage five times, all ending in failure. During his fifth attempt, his ship was blown far south to Hainan Island, and on the long return journey north, he suffered a severe illness and lost his eyesight completely.

Despite being

blind

and in his sixties, Master Jianzhen never gave up. On his

sixth attempt

in 753, he finally landed safely in Japan! Emperor Shōmu and Japanese monks welcomed him with grand respect. Jianzhen built the famous

Tōshōdai-ji

temple in Nara and established the official Buddhist ordination system in Japan.

Jianzhen brought much more than religious teachings; he brought a treasure trove of Chinese civilization. Accompanied by craftsmen, doctors, and artists, he introduced advanced Tang Dynasty architecture, sculpture, medicine, and calligraphy to Japan. He cured illnesses using Chinese herbal medicine and shaped Japanese culture so deeply that he is still honored in Japan today as the "Father of Japanese Medicine" and a supreme cultural ambassador.
